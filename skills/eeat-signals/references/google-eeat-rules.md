# Google's E-E-A-T rules, verbatim

**Provenance:** All quotes below are extracted directly from Google's official documents:
- **[QRG]** Search Quality Evaluator Guidelines, version **September 11, 2025** (services.google.com/fh/files/misc/hsw-sqrg.pdf → full guidelines at guidelines.raterhub.com), with section numbers
- **[HC]** "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" (developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content, last updated 2025-12-10)

Nothing here is paraphrased from third-party SEO commentary. When applying these rules, cite the section number.

---

## 1. The E-E-A-T definition [QRG §3.4]

> "Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trust (E-E-A-T) are all important considerations in PQ rating. **The most important member at the center of the E-E-A-T family is Trust.**"

> "**Trust:** Consider the extent to which the page is accurate, honest, safe, and reliable."

> "**Experience:** Consider the extent to which the content creator has the necessary first-hand or life experience for the topic. Many types of pages are trustworthy and achieve their purpose well when created by people with a wealth of personal experience."

> "**Expertise:** Consider the extent to which the content creator has the necessary knowledge or skill for the topic. Different topics require different levels and types of expertise to be trustworthy."

> "**Authoritativeness:** Consider the extent to which the content creator or the website is known as a go-to source for the topic."

> "Trust is the most important member of the E-E-A-T family because **untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T no matter how Experienced, Expert, or Authoritative they may seem.**"

> "**If a page is untrustworthy for any reason, it has low E-E-A-T.**" … "The website or content creator may not be a trustworthy source if there is a clear conflict of interest."

Trust is contextual [QRG §3.4]: "Online stores need secure online payment systems and reliable customer service. … Informational pages on clear YMYL topics must be accurate to prevent harm to people and society."

**Where raters look for E-E-A-T evidence [QRG §3.4]:** (1) "What the website or content creators say about themselves", (2) "What others say about the website or content creators", (3) "What is visible on the page, including the Main Content and sections such as reviews and comments".

[HC] confirms for ranking systems: "Of these aspects, **trust is most important**. The others contribute to trust, but content doesn't necessarily have to demonstrate all of them." And: "E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor."

## 2. YMYL: when the strict standard applies [QRG §2.3]

> "Some topics have a high risk of harm because content about these topics could significantly impact the health, financial stability, or safety of people, or the welfare or well-being of society. We call these topics 'Your Money or Your Life' or YMYL."

Categories: **YMYL Health or Safety; YMYL Financial Security** ("Topics that could damage a person's ability to support themselves and their families"); **YMYL Government, Civics & Society; YMYL Other**. Legal/consumer-rights content falls under Financial Security and Government/Civics.

**The official harm test:** "1. Would a careful person seek out experts or highly trusted sources to prevent harm? Could even minor inaccuracies cause harm? If yes, then the topic is likely YMYL. 2. Is the specific topic one that most people would be content with only casually consulting their friends about? If yes, the topic is likely not YMYL."

YMYL is a spectrum: "it may be helpful to think of topics as clear YMYL, definitely not YMYL or something in between."

Experience on YMYL [QRG §3.4.1]: "Pages that share first-hand life experience on clear YMYL topics may be considered to have high E-E-A-T as long as the content is trustworthy, safe, and consistent with well-established expert consensus. In contrast, **some types of YMYL information and advice must come from experts.**"

[HC]: "our systems give even more weight to content that aligns with strong E-E-A-T" for YMYL topics.

## 3. Main Content quality test [QRG §3.2]

> "For most pages, the quality of the MC can be determined by the amount of **effort, originality, and talent or skill** that went into the creation of the content. For informational pages and pages on YMYL topics, **accuracy and consistency with well established expert consensus** is important."

> "**Originality:** Consider the extent to which the content offers unique, original content that is not available on other websites. If other websites have similar content, consider whether the page is the original source."

> "**Accuracy:** For informational pages, consider the extent to which the content is factually accurate. For pages on YMYL topics, consider the extent to which the content is accurate and consistent with well-established expert consensus."

## 4. What gets rated Lowest [QRG §4.0–4.6]

> "Lowest quality pages are untrustworthy, deceptive, harmful to people or society, or have other highly undesirable characteristics."

E-E-A-T triggers for Lowest [§4.5]:
- "Multiple or significant factual inaccuracies on an informational page"
- "Inadequate information about the website or content creator for its purpose"
- "**If a page on YMYL topics is highly inexpert, it should be considered Untrustworthy and rated Lowest.**" [§4.5.2]
- "A complete lack of information about who is responsible for the website and its content for YMYL pages or other pages requiring trust"
- "A very negative reputation"

**Fake credentials rule [§4.5.3]** (deception → Lowest):
> "A webpage or website with 'fake' owner or content creator profiles. For example, AI generated content with made up 'author' profiles (AI generated images or deceptive creator descriptions) in order to make it appear that the content is written by people." And: "Factually inaccurate and deceptive information about creator expertise."

**Scaled/copied/AI content [§4.6.5–4.6.7]:**
> "Pages and websites made up of content created at scale with no original content or added value for users, should be rated Lowest, no matter how they are created."
> "The Lowest rating applies if all or almost all of the MC on the page … is copied, paraphrased, embedded, auto or AI generated, or reposted from other sources with little to no effort, little to no originality, and little to no added value."
> "**The use of Generative AI tools alone does not determine the level of effort or Page Quality rating.** Generative AI tools may be used for high quality and low quality content creation."
> "The Lowest rating is appropriate if there is little to no effort, little to no originality, and little to no added value for website visitors. The Low rating is appropriate if there is low effort, originality or added value."

## 5. Responsibility and contact information [QRG §2.5.2–2.5.3, §4.5.1]

> "Every page belongs to a website, and it should be clear: Who … is responsible for the website. Who … created the content on the page you are evaluating."

> "Contact information and customer service information are extremely important for websites that handle money, such as stores, banks, credit card companies, etc."

> "Any site that handles personal, private or sensitive data must provide extensive contact information. … **YMYL pages or websites that handle sensitive data with absolutely no information about the website or content creator should be rated Lowest.**"

Anonymity nuance: "personal websites may omit personal contact information"; aliases are acceptable for user-generated content — the responsibility rule scales with the trust the page demands.

## 6. Reputation research: how raters actually check [QRG §2.5, §3.3]

> "When there is disagreement between what the website or content creators say about themselves and what reputable independent sources say, **trust the independent sources.**"

> "Look for independent reviews, references, recommendations by experts, news articles, and other sources of credible information about the website. … News articles, Wikipedia articles, blog posts, magazine articles, forum discussions, and ratings from independent organizations can all be great sources of reputation information."

> "For YMYL topics, the reputation of a website should be judged by **what experts in the field have to say.**"

Rater search operators [§3.3.3]: `[brand -site:brand.com]`, `["brand.com" reviews -site:brand.com]`, `[brand site:en.wikipedia.org]`. Traffic data (e.g., Similarweb) is explicitly "not related to its reputation".

Professionals [§3.3.4]: "Educational degrees, peer validation, expert co-authors, and citations can be evidence of positive reputation information for professionals who publish their work."

Small sites [§3.3.5]: "small websites may have little or no reputation information. This is not indicative of high or low quality."

## 7. Google's self-assessment questions [HC, verbatim selection]

Content/quality: "Does the content provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?" · "Does the content provide substantial value when compared to other pages in search results?" · "Does the content have any easily-verified factual errors?"

Expertise: "Is this content written or reviewed by an expert or enthusiast who demonstrably knows the topic well?" · "Does the content present information in a way that makes you want to trust it, such as clear sourcing, evidence of the expertise involved, background about the author or the site that publishes it, such as through links to an author page or a site's About page?"

Who/How/Why framework [HC]:
- **Who:** "Do pages carry a byline, where one might be expected? Do bylines lead to further information about the author?" — "We strongly encourage adding accurate authorship information."
- **How:** "Is the use of automation, including AI-generation, self-evident to visitors through disclosures or in other ways?" — disclose "for content where someone might think 'How was this created?'"
- **Why:** "The 'why' should be that you're creating content primarily to help people. … If you use automation, including AI-generation, to produce content for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings, that's a violation of our spam policies."

Officially denied myths [HC]: word count ("Are you writing to a particular word count because you've heard or read that Google has a preferred word count? (No, we don't.)"); fake freshness ("Are you changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed?" — warning sign).

## 8. The quality ladder: Low → Medium → High → Highest [QRG §§5.0–8.4]

### Low quality [§5.0–5.1]

> "Low quality pages do not achieve their purpose well because they are lacking in an important dimension or have a problematic aspect." … "for any type of page, **a single Low quality attribute is enough** to use the Low quality rating."

Low triggers include: "MC is created without adequate effort, originality, talent, or skill" · "The page title is slightly misleading, shocking, or exaggerated" · "Unsatisfying amount of website information or information about the content creator" · "A mildly negative reputation" · "**Inadequate level of E-E-A-T for the purpose of the page.**"

**Lacking E-E-A-T [§5.1], verbatim examples:**
> "- The content creator lacks adequate experience, e.g. a restaurant review written by someone who has never eaten at the restaurant
> - The content creator lacks adequate expertise, e.g. an article about how to skydive written by someone with no expertise in the subject
> - The website or content creator is not an authoritative or trustworthy source for the topic of the page, e.g. tax form downloads provided on a cooking website.
> - The page or website is not trustworthy for its purpose, e.g. a shopping page with minimal customer service information"

> "**No other considerations such as positive reputation or the type of website can overcome a lack of E-E-A-T for the topic or purpose of the page.**"

Claims aren't evidence [§5.6]: "E-E-A-T assessments should be based on the MC itself, the information you find during reputation research, verifiable credentials, etc, **not just website or content creator claims of 'I'm an expert!'**"

### Medium quality [§6.0]

> "Medium pages have a beneficial purpose and achieve their purpose. There is nothing wrong with Medium quality pages." — "Adequate level of E-E-A-T"; reputation "not especially positive nor especially negative."

### High quality [§7.0–7.4]

> "High Quality Pages must demonstrate **at least one** of the following:" high effort/originality/talent/skill · positive reputation for the topic · "High level of E-E-A-T for the purpose of the page."

> "Have high standards! … 'typical' and 'average' pages on a topic generally have Medium (not High) quality MC." [§7.1] · "A page can still receive a High rating without reputation information." [§7.2]

**High E-E-A-T [§7.3], verbatim:** "Pages with High E-E-A-T are trustworthy or very trustworthy." · "**Experience is valuable for almost any topic.** Social media posts and forum discussions are often High quality when they involve people sharing their experience." · "**Expertise is required for satisfying content on a variety of topics**, from hobbies such as photography to YMYL topics such as tax preparation. … There are many types of informal expertise that may be visible in the MC itself." · "Government tax websites are an authoritative source for tax forms. Local businesses and organizations may be go-to sources for local information." · "**Trust is especially important** for High quality pages that involve processing financial transactions or cover YMYL topics." · "Think about the topic of the page. What kind of E-E-A-T is required for the page to achieve its purpose well?"

### Highest quality [§8.0–8.4]

> Must demonstrate at least one of: "very high level of effort, originality, talent, or skill" · "Very positive reputation" · "**Very high level of E-E-A-T for the purpose of the page.**"

**Very positive reputation [§8.2]:** "For YMYL topics, very positive reputation is often based on **recommendations from known experts or professional societies** appropriate to the topic of the page. Prestigious awards may be evidence as well." · "A page can still receive a Highest rating without reputation information."

**Very high E-E-A-T [§8.3], verbatim:**
> "Very high E-E-A-T is a distinguishing factor for Highest quality pages. A website or content creator who is the **uniquely authoritative, go-to source** for a topic has very high E-E-A-T. A content creator with a **wealth of experience** may be considered to have very high E-E-A-T for topics where experience is the primary factor in trust. A very high level of expertise can justify a very high E-E-A-T assessment. Very high E-E-A-T websites and content creators are **the most trusted sources on the internet** for a particular topic."

§8.4 examples: uniquely authoritative institutional sources (IRS for tax forms, kernel.org for Linux, the "only federally mandated" free credit report site) — and a recipe blogger who "documented her extensive experimentation" earning Highest **via experience alone**.

## Corrections vs. third-party claims

Two claims circulating in SEO commentary are **not** in the official Sept 11, 2025 document and must not be stated as Google rules: (1) a "Synthetic Authority" flag — the real rule is §4.5.3's fake-profiles/deceptive-expertise deception rating; (2) "AI content with no human author is rated Lowest regardless of accuracy" — the real rule is §4.6.6–4.6.7's effort/originality/added-value test, with Google explicitly stating AI use alone doesn't determine the rating.
