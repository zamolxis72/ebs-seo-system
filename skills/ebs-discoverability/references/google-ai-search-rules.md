# Google's official AI-search rules, verbatim

**Provenance:**
- **[AIO]** "Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search" — developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide, last updated **2026-07-10**
- **[AIF]** "AI features and your website" — developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features, last updated 2025-12-10

These govern Google AI Overviews / AI Mode / Gemini-grounded search. They do NOT govern ChatGPT, Perplexity, or other engines (see SKILL.md adaptation layer).

## 1. How sources get selected (the mechanism)

**Grounding/RAG [AIO]:** "A technique (also known as grounding) used to improve the quality, accuracy, and freshness of AI responses by relying on our core Search ranking systems to retrieve relevant, up-to-date web pages from our Search index. Our systems then review the specific information from those retrieved pages to generate a more reliable and helpful response, showing prominent, clickable links to relevant web pages that support the information in the response."

**Query fan-out [AIO/AIF]:** "A set of concurrent, related queries generated by the model to request more information and fetch additional relevant search results." [AIF]: "issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources … allowing us to display a wider and more diverse set of helpful links associated with the response than with a classic web search."

Consequence: AI features "are rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems" [AIO] — ranking eligibility is citation eligibility. "From Google Search's perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." [AIO]

## 2. Eligibility (strict requirements)

[AIF, bolded in source]: "**There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary.**" · "a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, fulfilling the Search technical requirements."

[AIO adds, 2026]: "a site must be included in Search generative AI features in Search Console to be eligible for display in generative AI features." Check this toggle for clients.

Caveat: "Indexing and serving aren't guaranteed."

## 3. What Google says to do

[AIF] best-practices list, verbatim bullets: "Ensuring that crawling is allowed in robots.txt, and by any CDN or hosting infrastructure" · "Making your content easily findable through internal links" · "Providing a great page experience for users" · "Making sure that important content is available in textual form" · "Supporting your textual content with high-quality images and videos, when applicable" · "Making sure your structured data matches the visible text on the page" · "Checking that your Merchant Center and Business Profile information is up-to-date."

[AIO] top recommendation: "Creating content that people find unique, compelling, and useful will likely influence your website's presence in generative AI search in the long run more than any of the other suggestions in this guide."

[AIO] on uniqueness: "**Providing a unique point of view**: Our AI systems take a look at a variety of sources, so it can be helpful to have a unique viewpoint that stands out … Don't just recycle what others on the internet have already said, or could easily be produced by a generative AI model." Avoid "commodity content (for example, something like '7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers')" in favor of "unique expert or experienced takes that go beyond common knowledge." Test: "Is this content that my visitors would find satisfying?"

[AIO] spam warning: creating pages for every query variation "primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses in Google Search violates Google's scaled content abuse spam policy."

[AIO] multimodal: "our generative AI search features can bring in relevant images and video … If you're already following our image SEO best practices and video SEO documentation, you're already optimizing for generative AI search."

## 4. Official mythbusting [AIO] — never recommend these as Google requirements

- **llms.txt / AI files / Markdown**: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown … Google Search ignores them."
- **Chunking**: "There's no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces for AI to better understand it … There's no ideal page length."
- **Writing 'for AI'**: "You don't need to write in a specific way just for generative AI search. AI systems can understand synonyms and general meanings."
- **Inauthentic mentions**: "seeking inauthentic 'mentions' across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem."
- **Special schema**: "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add. However, it's a good idea to continue using it as part of your overall SEO strategy, as it helps with being eligible for rich results."
- **Third-party tools**: "No third-party tool has access to our internal ranking or AI systems."

## 5. Controls and measurement

Controls [AIF]: robots.txt governs access; "To limit the information shown from your pages in Search, use `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, or `noindex` controls." (So an aggressive `max-snippet` can suppress AI citation display — check it isn't set accidentally.) Google-Extended governs non-Search AI training.

Measurement: [AIO] "use the Generative AI performance report in Search Console" (2026 report); [AIF] AI clicks "are higher quality (meaning, users are more likely to spend more time on the site)."

## 6. Honest scope note

Google's mythbusting says structure isn't *required* for Google's AI. It does not say clear structure is useless, and it does not govern ChatGPT/Perplexity/Claude, which retrieve and quote differently. Anything recommended beyond this file must be labeled as adaptation-layer practice, not Google rule.
